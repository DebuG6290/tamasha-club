# Games from the table to the projector

Research and design proposals, 21 October 2026. Only **Ek Jaisa!** below is implemented in this update. Proposed adaptations have not been playtested. Use original names, illustrations, audio and written rules when implementing them.

## Built: Ek Jaisa!

Inspired by the visual-search mechanic in [Dobble's publisher rules](https://www.asmodee.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-play-dobble). Original 31-symbol artwork includes chai, patang, auto, jalebi, cricket bat and chappal, alongside simple recognisable objects. No reading is needed during a search.

- **2–8 people, individual scores**, also available in Club Cup.
- Projector shows one circular card with six symbols. Each phone gets its own six-symbol card. Every private card and the projected card share exactly one symbol.
- First valid tap received by the server earns **1 point** and ends the search. Wrong taps cost **1 point**, never below zero, and lock the phone for one second. No elimination or lost turns.
- **15 rounds**. Three-second readiness countdown, search up to 18 seconds, 2.4-second reveal. Highest score wins; ties share a rank. Rounds advance automatically; host can pause.
- Illustrations change position, size and mild rotation between cards; each identity keeps its shape and colour. A shuffled 31-card finite-geometry deck guarantees the one-common-symbol rule; central cards do not repeat within a game. Private card deals and symbol identity permutation vary each game.
- Projector: large uncluttered shared card, current winner and matching illustration, live individual scores, round dots. Three consecutive wins trigger a small “Phir se?!” line, with no scoring advantage.
- Phone: upright, two-column by three-row card. Whole tiles are tap targets; no dragging, tiny hit areas or confirmation button. Landscape becomes three columns by two rows. On reveal each phone highlights its own correct symbol, even when someone else's winning symbol differs.
- Audio: short papery deal, three-note correct-match pluck, soft two-note unclaimed-round cue. Optional effects play on the shared screen; optional phone feedback confirms local touch and server result. Haptics depend on browser/device support; no required sound-only cues. Reduced-motion preference disables shake/pop animation.
- Server owns scores, card validation, stage deadlines, duplicate-input protection and winner selection. Projector and rival phones do not receive another player's card. Live taps use WebSocket with HTTP fallback; reconnect retains the same card and score.

**Tradeoffs:** looking between phone and projector is the intended skill, but needs a real-room legibility test. Different personal cards can differ in search difficulty despite identical symbol counts. Network delay affects first-arrival ordering; this is a casual room game, with no claim of calibrated competitive timing. Three-second inter-round countdowns help everyone look back up. Recheck round pacing with a real group before adding more complexity.

## Selection criteria

Prioritise a rule explainable with one demonstrated turn, large phone controls, projected consequences everyone understands, quick recovery after mistakes, and random/constructed content that remains varied after one play. Physical balance, stacking or grabbing games do not automatically survive conversion: if touch removes the enjoyable skill, retain the reference only as research. A longer list is not a better collection; **Ghanti! + Dabba Shuffle** would add clearer variety than three more spotting games.
